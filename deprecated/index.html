Che-Yung Shen

I work with Prof. Aydogan Ozcan on diffractive optics, computational imaging, and deep-learning inverse design. Before UCLA I received my B.S. in Optics and Photonics from National Central University (2017) and my M.S. in Electro-optical Engineering from National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University (2019).

I develop AI-enabled optical systems that unlock new capabilities in imaging, sensing, and information processing, with applications in optical computing, displays, and digital pathology.

My work has appeared in Nature Communications, Light: Science & Applications, Advanced Photonics, and Science Advances. I interned at Meta Reality Labs on deep learning for VR optics, and I am a 2025 Amazon Fellow at the Amazon–UCLA Science Hub.

Selected work

Nature Communications · 2024

All-optical phase conjugation without digital processing

Passive diffractive layers, trained with deep learning, approximate optical phase conjugation through a shallow volume. Compact, multi-wavelength wavefront correction with no SLM loop or extra compute—useful framing for aberration control and AR/VR optical paths.

JSID 2020 · SID 2019 · US 11,340,555 · LSA 2026

Holographic HUD / 3D display and LCOS SLMs

Display-industry work on analog LCOS devices for AR, holographic head-up image enhancement, and an issued patent (US 11,340,555) on an adjustably multifocal 3D holographic display system. For recent related works please also see super-resolution image projection over an extended depth of field (LSA 2026).

BME Frontiers · 2026

Deep learning pathology using lensfree holography

Compact lensfree holography plus deep learning for automated HER2 scoring of breast-cancer tissue, with Bayesian uncertainty so low-confidence cases can be left unscored. Large-FOV imaging (~1,250 mm2) without lenses or mechanical focusing; 84.9% four-class and 94.8% binary accuracy on a blinded test set.

Light: Science & Applications · 2025

Wafer-scale unidirectional imagers in the visible light

First-author collaboration with Broadcom: a polarization-insensitive visible imager that forms an image in one direction and blocks the reverse path, fabricated by wafer-scale lithography on fused silica (~0.5 billion nanoscale phase features per wafer). Built for high-throughput manufacturing, sensing, and privacy optics—not a one-off lab demo.

Advanced Photonics · 2024

3D quantitative phase imaging by wavelength multiplexing

A diffractive processor maps phase objects at different depths onto wavelength-encoded intensity patterns, so an intensity-only sensor recovers multiplane QPI without iterative phase retrieval. Compact on-chip phase sensing rather than a bulky interferometer.
